# Consolidated Trading Rulebook from Both Transcript Sets

This document merges the two transcript sets into one structured rulebook, highlights inconsistencies, and places **all double Bollinger Band advice in one dedicated section**.

## Source note
This is a reconstruction from noisy transcripts with clear ASR / pronunciation errors. Rules below are grouped into:
- **Directly stated rules** = rules clearly expressed by the speaker
- **Inferred mirror rules** = the opposite-side version of a clearly stated rule, included only where the framework obviously implies symmetry
- **Claimed stats** = performance or probability claims made by the speaker, not independently verified

---

# 1. High-level framework shared across both sets

## 1.1 Core worldview

1. Trading should be kept **simple**, not cluttered with many indicators.
2. Indicators are **tools**, not autonomous buy/sell buttons.
3. Candle structure matters more than indicator overload.
4. Most real entries fall into only **two categories**:
   - **reversal**
   - **breakout**
5. A trader should avoid entering in vague “no man’s land” between reversal and breakout.
6. Confirmation matters more than prediction.
7. Candle **close** is repeatedly emphasized as the key confirmation point.

## 1.2 Repeated process logic

Across the transcripts, the speaker repeatedly uses this sequence:

1. Read the **higher-timeframe context** first.
2. Determine whether the setup is likely **reversal** or **breakout**.
3. Use indicators only to clarify the price structure.
4. Wait for **candle close** or follow-through confirmation.
5. Enter only where the **invalidation point is clear**.
6. Favor setups where **risk is small relative to reward**.

# 2. Candle-reading rules from the combined transcripts

## 2.1 Four-part candle checklist

The speaker says every candle should be read through four basic components:

1. **Color**
   - Bullish if close > open
   - Bearish if close < open
2. **Body**
   - Large body = strong directional push / stronger agreement
   - Small body = weak push / less conviction
3. **Wicks**
   - Wicks show where price moved but failed to hold
   - Lower wick = rejection of lower prices
   - Upper wick = rejection of higher prices
4. **Total candle size / range**
   - Very large range = dominant intent / likely continuation
   - Small messy candles = accumulation, compression, indecision

## 2.2 Wick rules

1. A **long lower wick** suggests buyers rejected lower prices.
2. A **long upper wick** suggests sellers rejected higher prices.
3. Wick rejection is a major clue for **reversal setups**.
4. A long wick can define a **support/resistance zone**, **entry zone**, or **future reaction target**.
5. The speaker also claims price often later comes back to “fill” wick areas.

## 2.3 Body / impulse rules

1. A **large impulse candle** signals stronger directional intent.
2. A strong candle with **little or no opposite wick** is treated as higher-quality continuation evidence.
3. Do **not** enter while the impulse candle is still forming.
4. Wait until the impulse candle **fully closes**, then act.

## 2.4 Hammer-style reversal rules

1. A hammer-style candle with a **long lower wick** after a flush lower is treated as a key reversal signal.
2. One explicit filter: if price flushes lower, then the wick retraces **more than 50%** and the candle closes, that candle becomes the key reversal candle.
3. Enter on the **next candle**, not before the hammer closes.
4. Do not place the stop exactly at the hammer low in noisy conditions; give it some buffer.

## 2.5 Gap rules

1. A **gap up** above the previous close is treated as bullish context.
2. On a daily reversal setup, the speaker says to:
   - wait for the daily candle to close,
   - then next morning, if there is a gap up,
   - drop to the 1-hour chart,
   - if the first hourly candle closes bullish, enter long.
3. If price gaps up but the first hourly candle closes bearish, do not enter immediately.
4. Gap failure invalidates the read; the stop is tied to the prior day’s key level.

# 3. General trend / structure rules from both sets

## 3.1 Trend filtering

1. Check higher timeframes first: usually **1H, 4H, daily**, and sometimes weekly.
2. Prefer trading **with** the larger trend rather than against it.
3. The speaker repeatedly warns against fading a strong trend just because price touched a band.
4. Trend direction is often assessed using:
   - Bollinger reaction,
   - candle behavior,
   - 20 MA slope,
   - sometimes both 20 and 60 MA slope.

## 3.2 Support / resistance / supply / demand

1. Band touches alone are not enough.
2. Entries should also be checked against:
   - previous highs,
   - previous lows,
   - support,
   - resistance,
   - supply zones,
   - demand zones,
   - day high / day low in the faster breakout methods.

## 3.3 Event risk

1. Major events can invalidate simple band-touch logic.
2. The speaker specifically warns about trading simplistic reversals into major releases.
3. Around CPI / jobs / rate-type events, he prefers to wait until direction becomes clearer.
4. One explicit rule stated later: often stay out for roughly **1 hour after the data drop**, then trade the established direction.

# 4. 20 moving average pullback / scalping rules (non-double-Bollinger section)

This section is separate from the double Bollinger system.

## 4.1 Setup

1. Use the **1-minute chart**.
2. Use the **20 moving average**.
3. Trend is defined by the 20 MA slope:
   - up = uptrend
   - down = downtrend
4. The edge comes from **price deviation away from the 20 MA**.

## 4.2 Scalping logic

1. When price stretches too far from the 20 MA, a pullback becomes more likely.
2. The trade is to take a **small retracement**, not to demand the full reversal.
3. Primary scalp target: **25% retracement**.
4. Optional extension: secure 25%, then try to hold some for **50% retracement**.

## 4.3 Claimed probability structure

The speaker claims approximate retracement probabilities:

- 100% retracement ≈ 10%
- 75% retracement ≈ 20%
- 50% retracement ≈ 60%
- 25% retracement ≈ 90%

These are **claimed backtest stats**, not independently verified.

## 4.4 Continuation vs reversal logic

1. If the bounce remains **below 50% retracement**, the original trend is treated as intact.
2. If retracement goes **past 75%**, reversal becomes plausible.
3. If a 75%+ retracement occurs and the next pullback fails to make a new low/high, he shifts from scalp logic to swing-reversal logic.

## 4.5 Risk logic

1. Scalping prioritizes **win rate** over huge RR.
2. For re-entry or reversal expansion attempts, risk should stay small.
3. One explicit risk number appears: cap a new attempt at roughly **1%**.

# 5. All double Bollinger Band trade advice in one section

This section consolidates **every double Bollinger-specific instruction** from the transcripts.

## 5.1 Indicator setup for the classic “double Bollinger” system

The speaker’s classic double Bollinger setup is:

### Band A: fast custom band
- **Period = 4**
- **Standard deviation = 4**
- **Source = Open**
- **Color = Red**

### Band B: standard band
- **Period = 20**
- **Standard deviation = 2**
- **Color = White**

He refers to this as **double Bollinger** or **double B**.

## 5.2 Purpose of the two bands

The stated purpose is:

1. The **20,2 band** shows the standard market envelope most traders recognize.
2. The **4,4 on open band** gives a faster short-term envelope.
3. Using both together is supposed to distinguish:
   - ordinary mean reversion,
   - failed breakouts,
   - real breakouts,
   - safer reversal zones.

## 5.3 Simplification rule

1. Use **only two Bollinger Bands**.
2. Do **not** clutter the chart with other indicators.
3. More indicators are treated as judgment-clouding noise.

## 5.4 How he says to read double Bollinger

He says to watch three things:

1. **Width of the bands**
2. **How expanded the bands are**
3. **Whether the bands are contracted**

The bands are treated as a visual way to identify:
- safe entry zones,
- stretched conditions,
- likely reversion,
- possible breakout continuation.

## 5.5 Core decision tree of double Bollinger

When price reaches the bands, there are only **two scenarios**:

1. **Reversal**
2. **Breakout**

That is the whole decision tree.

---

## 5.6 Double Bollinger reversal rules

### Type 1 reversal: immediate rejection

**Directly stated short example**

1. Price rises and touches both:
   - the upper fast band,
   - the upper standard band.
2. The candle rejects from that area.
3. It leaves an **upper wick**.
4. The candle body closes **back inside** the band.
5. That means price failed to make a new high.
6. That candle becomes a **sell signal**.
7. Trader adopts a bearish bias and looks short.

### Implied mirror long version

By symmetry, the implied long version is:

1. Price touches both lower bands.
2. Candle rejects lower prices.
3. Lower wick forms.
4. Candle closes back inside the band.
5. That becomes a long reversal signal.

The long version is not stated as clearly, but the framework strongly implies it.

---

### Type 2 reversal: false breakout that fails

This is one of the more important rules in the transcript.

#### Short version

1. Price touches both upper bands.
2. It even closes above them.
3. But there is **previous high / resistance / supply** directly ahead.
4. Price does **not** truly clear that resistance.
5. The next candle closes bearish, confirming seller control.
6. That is classified as a **type 2 reversal**, not a real breakout.
7. Entry is taken only **after** that next candle confirms seller dominance.
8. If the confirming candle prints an upper wick and closes bearish, that is the cue to short.

#### Implied long mirror

1. Price breaks below both lower bands.
2. But there is support / prior low / demand immediately below.
3. Price fails to truly break support.
4. Next candle confirms bullish rejection.
5. That is treated as failed downside breakout and reversal long.

---

## 5.7 Double Bollinger breakout rules

### Real upside breakout

A real upside breakout requires more than piercing the bands.

#### Long breakout conditions

1. Price pushes through **both upper bands**.
2. Price also breaks the **previous high / resistance / supply area**.
3. The candle **closes above** that level.
4. That is treated as a **real breakout**.
5. That is the moment to go long.

### Real downside breakout

#### Short breakout conditions

1. Price breaks lower through the relevant lower band structure.
2. It also breaks support / prior low.
3. The move happens with a **large expansion candle**.
4. That is treated as confirmed downside breakout.
5. Entry is short with a tight invalidation level.

---

## 5.8 Confirmation rules inside the double Bollinger framework

The speaker repeatedly says double Bollinger must **not** be used in isolation.

### Required or strongly preferred confirmations

1. **Previous highs**
2. **Previous lows**
3. **Support / resistance**
4. **Supply zones**
5. **Candle close**
6. **Wick rejection** for reversals
7. **Strong body / expansion** for breakouts

### Special caution

A candle above the band is **not automatically** a breakout.
It can still be:
- a test,
- a trap,
- a fake breakout,
- a reversal setup.

---

## 5.9 Stop-loss and target logic in the double Bollinger system

### Explicit stop rule

One explicit rule stated:

1. If entering at the **lower band**, and a candle breaks below that lower band, cut the loss.

### Explicit target rule

1. The **opposite band** can be used as the target.
2. Example given:
   - entry near lower band,
   - stop below lower band,
   - target upper band.

### Breakout stop logic

1. For confirmed breakouts, the stop should remain **clear and tight**.
2. The exact stop formula is not fully specified, but it is tied to the breakout invalidation point.

---

## 5.10 Risk/reward logic in the double Bollinger system

1. The speaker says this structure naturally creates **at least 3:1 risk-to-reward** in many cases.
2. He repeatedly says you should be able to explain:
   - why you entered,
   - why you exited,
   - where the stop is,
   - why the reward justifies the risk.
3. He repeatedly emphasizes **clear logic** over random band-touch trading.

---

## 5.11 Claimed statistical beliefs inside the double Bollinger framework

These are speaker claims, not verified facts:

1. With a standard single Bollinger Band, price returns inside the band about **80%** of the time.
2. The remaining ~20% is where breakout risk appears.
3. Using a single band gave about **55% win rate** in his claimed test.
4. Using double Bollinger increased that to about **78%**.
5. He also claims better average payoff with double Bollinger.

Treat all of those as **unverified promotional claims**.

---

## 5.12 Trend sequencing rule inside the double Bollinger system

One of the more important structural rules he gives:

1. First, identify the **breakout trade**.
2. Then, after the breakout is real, trade the **pullback**.
3. Only then trade the **trend continuation**.

He specifically warns against blindly chasing a late trend move without first validating the breakout.

## 5.13 Best combined reconstruction of the double Bollinger playbook

### Reversal short

1. Price touches both upper bands.
2. Watch candle behavior.
3. If candle rejects and closes back inside, especially with upper wick, classify as reversal.
4. Check that prior resistance / previous high is nearby or still holding.
5. Enter short only after rejection is confirmed.
6. Stop above invalidation / failed-high region.
7. Initial target can be opposite band or next support.

### Reversal long

1. Price touches both lower bands.
2. Watch candle behavior.
3. If candle rejects and closes back inside, especially with lower wick, classify as reversal.
4. Check that support / previous low / demand is holding.
5. Enter long after confirmation.
6. Stop below invalidation.
7. Target opposite band or next resistance.

### Breakout long

1. Price breaks both upper bands.
2. It also clears previous high / resistance / supply.
3. Candle closes above that structure.
4. Treat as real breakout.
5. Enter long.
6. Stop below invalidation.
7. Then look for the later pullback and continuation sequence.

### Breakout short

1. Price breaks both lower bands.
2. It also clears support / prior low.
3. Move is driven by a strong expansion candle.
4. Treat as real downside breakout.
5. Enter short.
6. Stop above invalidation.
7. Then handle pullback / continuation sequence if trend persists.

# 6. The later Bollinger-based buy/sell-band system (not the classic double-Bollinger 4,4 + 20,2 setup)

This is a different Bollinger variation introduced later.

## 6.1 Higher-timeframe context

On 1H / 4H / daily he says he uses:
- default Bollinger Bands
- 20 MA

## 6.2 Intraday custom buy band

For buy setups on fast charts, he builds two Bollinger Bands:
- price base = **High**, MA = **EMA**
- price base = **High**, MA = **WMA**
- colors = green

## 6.3 Intraday custom sell band

For sell setups on fast charts, he builds two Bollinger Bands:
- price base = **Low**, MA = **EMA**
- price base = **Low**, MA = **WMA**
- colors = red

## 6.4 Usage rules

1. If higher timeframe is bullish, only use the **green buy band**.
2. If higher timeframe is bearish, only use the **red sell band**.
3. If 20 MA slopes up and price touches the bottom of the green band, that is a buy area.
4. If 20 MA slopes down and price touches the top of the red band, that is a sell area.
5. Candle quality then decides whether it is reversal or breakout.
6. A true breakout also requires a break of the **day high / day low**.

# 7. Inconsistencies and tensions across the transcript sets

This section compares the two sets and identifies inconsistencies, contradictions, or at least strategy drift.

## 7.1 “Only two indicators, no exceptions” vs multiple other tools later

### Early claim
He says he has used **only two indicators** for over 10 years and takes every trade with just those two Bollinger Bands.

### Later material
He later introduces or relies on:
- candle-only reading,
- 20 moving average,
- 20 and 60 moving averages,
- default Bollinger on higher timeframe,
- custom buy/sell Bollinger bands,
- higher timeframe trend filters,
- day high / day low filters,
- event filters.

### Conclusion
This is a **clear inconsistency**.
The later material shows a larger decision framework than “only two indicators, no exceptions.”

---

## 7.2 Double Bollinger exclusivity vs later custom Bollinger variants

### Early system
The classic system is:
- 4,4 Bollinger on open
- 20,2 standard Bollinger

### Later system
The later Bollinger setup changes substantially:
- default Bollinger on HTF,
- high-based EMA + WMA Bollinger pair for buys,
- low-based EMA + WMA Bollinger pair for sells.

### Conclusion
This is not just a small tweak. It is effectively a **different Bollinger framework**.
So either:
1. he uses multiple systems, or
2. the “only two indicators” claim is oversimplified / promotional.

---

## 7.3 Mean reversion preference vs breakout preference

### Early emphasis
He often praises reversal entries from extremes because:
- risk is smaller,
- psychology is easier,
- late trend entries are dangerous.

### Later emphasis
He also says:
- experienced traders often prefer breakouts,
- big expansion candles are highly actionable,
- entering after accumulation expansion can be safer than guessing bottoms.

### Conclusion
This is not a direct contradiction, but it is a **tension**.
His actual position seems to be:
- reversals are great when clearly confirmed at extremes,
- breakouts are also valid if structure and candle strength confirm them.

---

## 7.4 “Band touch means reverse 80–90% of the time” vs repeated warnings not to trade band touches blindly

### Claimed stats
He gives very strong reversion claims:
- ~80% return inside band,
- >90% in some broad descriptions.

### Practical guidance
He also repeatedly says:
- do not buy just because lower band touched,
- do not sell just because upper band touched,
- you need support/resistance, structure, event awareness, candle quality, HTF trend.

### Conclusion
This is partly a contradiction in **presentation**, though not necessarily in method.
The likely true operational rule is:
- band touch alone is **not sufficient**, even if he claims it mean-reverts often.

---

## 7.5 “Candles alone are enough” vs “Bollinger makes decisions easier”

### Candle-first message
One transcript says candlesticks alone can let you read the market and other indicators are just extra confirmation.

### Bollinger-first message
Another transcript says Bollinger is the critical tool that makes decisions objective and prevents trading blindness.

### Conclusion
This is a philosophical inconsistency.
The best reconciliation is:
- he wants candles for final interpretation,
- Bollinger for structure / location,
- moving averages for trend.

In practice, that is **not** “candles alone.”

---

## 7.6 Target philosophy inconsistency: 3:1 minimum vs 25% quick scalp

### Double Bollinger section
He often emphasizes **3:1 risk-reward** or better.

### Scalping section
He later says scalping should focus on **25% retracement capture** and very high win rate rather than large RR.

### Conclusion
These are two different trade models:
- swing / structured reversal-breakout model
- quick deviation scalp model

Not a contradiction if separated, but inconsistent if marketed as one unified system.

2. Candle quality decides which one it is.

# 8. Best merged reconstruction of the speaker’s actual overall method

If the marketing language is stripped away, the combined method appears to be:

## 8.1 Context layer

1. Check weekly / daily / 4H / 1H trend.
2. Use MA slope, Bollinger location, and prior structure.
3. Avoid fighting strong higher-timeframe trends.
4. Be cautious around major macro events.

## 8.2 Location layer

1. Use Bollinger structure to locate stretched price zones.
2. In the classic system, use double Bollinger (4,4 open + 20,2).
3. In the later system, use custom buy/sell bands on the fast chart.
4. Use support/resistance / day high-low / supply-demand to decide whether band interaction matters.

## 8.3 Trigger layer

1. Use candles to decide reversal vs breakout.
2. Reversal clues:
   - wick rejection,
   - close back inside,
   - failure to clear structure.
3. Breakout clues:
   - strong body candle,
   - expansion,
   - break of previous high/low or day high/low,
   - close beyond structure.

## 8.4 Execution layer

1. Wait for the candle close.
2. Enter with clear invalidation.
3. Keep stops at the point where the read is proven wrong.
4. For scalps, take smaller reliable chunks.
5. For validated breakouts / reversals, aim for larger asymmetry.

# 9. Short practical summary

## Most consistent rules across everything

1. Keep the chart simple.
2. Read candles, not just indicator labels.
3. Every setup is either **reversal** or **breakout**.
4. Wait for **close confirmation**.
5. Band touch alone is not enough.
6. Use higher-timeframe trend and structure as filters.
7. Use wick rejection for reversals.
8. Use strong body / expansion for breakouts.
9. Keep losses small with clear invalidation.
10. Do not chase late entries in strong moves without confirmation.

# 10. Final takeaway on the double Bollinger advice

If all the double Bollinger material is reduced to its essential logic, it becomes:

1. Plot:
   - **4,4 Bollinger on open**
   - **20,2 Bollinger**
2. Use them to identify stretched price and structure.
3. Treat every band interaction as either:
   - **reversal** if price rejects and fails,
   - **breakout** if price expands through and clears structure.
4. Do not decide from the bands alone.
5. Use candle quality, prior highs/lows, support/resistance, and close confirmation.
6. Use opposite band / structure for targets and invalidation for stops.
7. Prefer the sequence:
   - breakout validated,
   - pullback forms,
   - continuation trade comes next.
